01

What Is Phishing?

A technical deep-dive into social engineering via deceptive digital communication.

⚠ Threat Reality Check

Over 90% of successful cyberattacks begin with a phishing email. Even technically sophisticated IT staff are targeted — often via highly personalised spear-phishing campaigns that leverage OSINT from LinkedIn, GitHub, and internal directories.

🎣

Definition

Phishing is a social engineering attack that uses deceptive messages to trick users into revealing credentials, installing malware, or transferring funds.

🎯

Why IT Staff Are Targeted

IT staff have elevated privileges, system access, and are trusted by peers — making them high-value targets for attackers using credential harvesting.

📈

Scale of the Problem

Phishing attempts increase by ~150% YoY. AI-generated phishing emails now achieve higher click rates than manually crafted ones.

ℹ Attack Chain

Phishing is often the initial access stage in a larger kill chain: Reconnaissance → Phishing → Credential Theft → Lateral Movement → Data Exfiltration / Ransomware.

⚡ IT-Specific Threat: Helpdesk Impersonation

Attackers call IT helpdesks pretending to be employees and request password resets, MFA disabling, or VPN credential sharing. Always verify identity through a secondary, out-of-band channel.

🤖

AI-Powered Phishing

LLMs now generate grammatically perfect, context-aware phishing emails. Gone are the days of "Dear sir/madam" typos as detection signals.

🔗

QR Code Phishing (Quishing)

QR codes in emails bypass URL scanners and link proxies — a growing vector targeting mobile devices and bypassing endpoint protection.

Anatomy of a Phishing Email

Hover over highlighted elements to reveal red flags in this realistic spear-phishing example.

ℹ Interactive Exercise

This is a simulated phishing email targeting an IT administrator. Hover over the underlined red elements to reveal annotations.

FROM: it-support@micros0ft-helpdesk.com FAKE DOMAIN
TO: john.admin@yourcompany.com
SUBJECT: [URGENT] Your Azure AD admin account will be suspended in 24 hours URGENCY TACTIC
DATE: Mon, 12 May 2025 09:17:42 +0000

Dear John,


Our security systems have detected multiple failed login attempts FEAR TRIGGER on your Azure Active Directory administrator account.


To avoid suspension, please verify your identity immediately by signing in at:


https://azure-admin-verify.micros0ft-helpdesk.com/login?token=aHR0cHM6Ly9ldmlsLmNvbQ ⚠ FAKE URL — 'micros0ft' uses zero not 'o' LOOK-ALIKE DOMAIN


This link expires in 24 hours FALSE DEADLINE. If you believe this is an error, contact your IT security team.


Regards,
Microsoft Azure Security Team IMPERSONATION
© 2025 Microsoft Corporation. One Microsoft Way, Redmond, WA. FAKE FOOTER

Key red flags identified:

  • 🔴
    Look-alike domain"micros0ft" uses digit zero (0) instead of letter 'o' — a classic homograph/typosquatting attack.
  • 🔴
    Urgency & fear"24 hours", "suspended", "failed attempts" — pressure tactics designed to bypass rational thinking.
  • 🔴
    Suspicious URL with encoded tokenBase64-encoded redirect parameter conceals the true destination from link preview tools.
  • 🟡
    Generic greetingEven with correct name, sender doesn't know your role title or internal team structure — a sign of OSINT-limited targeting.
  • 🟡
    Mismatched sender domainMicrosoft sends account alerts from @microsoft.com — never a third-party helpdesk domain.
04

Technical Defenses

Best practices and controls for IT staff to detect, prevent, and respond to phishing.

EMAIL HEADER ANALYSIS
# Check SPF, DKIM, DMARC in email headers:
Received-SPF: fail (domain of attacker.com does not designate)
DKIM-Signature: NONE ← no signing = suspicious
Authentication-Results: dmarc=fail action=quarantine
 
# Verify sender IP reputation:
$ whois 185.220.101.42 | grep -i "org\|country"
$ curl https://api.abuseipdb.com/api/v2/check?ipAddress=185.220.101.42
✔
Enforce MFA (Phishing-resistant)

Use FIDO2 / hardware security keys (YubiKey). TOTP is bypassable via real-time phishing proxies (Evilginx2).

✔
Deploy DMARC / DKIM / SPF

Configure strict DMARC policies (p=reject) on all domains to prevent email spoofing of your organisation.

✔
Email Sandboxing

Route attachments and links through sandboxes (Proofpoint, Defender for O365) before delivery to inbox.

✔
URL Rewriting & Scanning

Rewrite all inbound links through a proxy that scans in real-time at click time, not just at delivery.

✔
Verify via Out-of-Band Channel

Always confirm suspicious requests by calling the sender on a known number — not a number in the email.

✔
Zero Trust Architecture

Assume breach. Segment networks, enforce least-privilege, and require continuous identity verification beyond the perimeter.

✔
Incident Response Playbook

Have a documented, rehearsed IR procedure specifically for phishing: isolate, preserve, report, remediate.

✔
Regular Red Team Simulations

Use tools like GoPhish to run internal phishing simulations. Track metrics, identify weak links, and train in context.

⚡ Advanced: Real-Time Phishing Proxy (AiTM)

Adversary-in-the-Middle (AiTM) attacks (via Evilginx2, Modlishka) can steal session cookies even after MFA is completed. Defense: enforce hardware FIDO2 keys and Conditional Access policies that evaluate device posture continuously.
